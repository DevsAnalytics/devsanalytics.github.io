Six habits that keep your payments safe

None of these need special software, just a pause at the right moment.

Don't fall for fake payment instructions

When you receive updated payment instructions, take extra precaution. Call a known person at the requesting company, using a number you already have on file. Never use the number in the email or letter.

Call and confirm the change is valid.

Be careful what you share online

Pet names, schools attended, links to family members, and birthday celebrations can give bad actors exactly what they need to pass verification questions.

Examine email addresses and links

Humans have been, and will continue to be, an essential part of the scammer's attack vector. Always keep the human firewall running.

Use two-factor authentication everywhere

Username and password are one level. Step-up authentication is the next. Configured properly, a fraudster has to compromise both before reaching a sensitive account.

Do not share login credentials

Not with a coworker, a friend, or someone claiming to be support. Each person gets their own access.

Keep software up to date

Install the latest update as soon as it is available.

Email case checker

Email addresses are usually treated as case-insensitive, so matching them is easier when they're all lowercase. Paste one in to check it.
